INT. ARCHIVE LIBRARY - CORNELIA'S ESTATE - NIGHT
\n
Sleek 1920s jazz plays softly from hidden acoustic panels. The room is immaculate—warm mahogany, glass cases holding real paper books, and a stabilized 70-degree micro-climate while a bitter December rain hits the terrace outside.
\n
CORNELIA (26) adjusts Julian’s velvet bow tie. Her touch is light, practiced, and perfectly polite.
\n
CORNELIA
\n
Your father’s already on his second glass of vintage synthetic. He told my dad you’re considering the North Grid merger.
\n
JULIAN (28)
\n
(Staring past her out the window)
\n
My dad says a lot of things when his blood-sugar stabilizes.
\n
CORNELIA
\n
It’s a good look for you, Julian. Being serious. You’ve been... drifting since the summer. People notice.
\n
Julian takes a slow breath. He feels a familiar, heavy tightness in his chest—the quiet, creeping fatigue that always comes when he’s around her for more than twenty minutes. She isn't mean; she's just empty. A human vacuum sucking the air out of his lungs.
\n
JULIAN
\n
I was at the hospital until 6 AM with my mother, Cornelia.
\n
CORNELIA
\n
(Softens slightly, but her eyes stay cool)
\n
I know. It’s sweet. Truly. But she’d want you out tonight. Turning 2100 in a hospital room isn't an strategy. You need to be seen in the room. With me.
\n
Julian nods numbly. He looks at his reflection in the dark glass. He can practically feel his own battery draining into her floorboards.
\n

INT. GRAND FOYER - NIGHT
